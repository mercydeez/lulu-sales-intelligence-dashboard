# Design Principles

## 1. Tell The Truth First

The interface must never become more persuasive than the underlying system.

- units stay units
- AED stays derived value
- permissions stay explicit
- AI confidence stays honest
- stale or missing data stays visible

## 2. Home Must Answer The Day

The first authenticated screen should answer:

1. what changed
2. what matters
3. what needs me
4. what AI recommends
5. where to go next

If a page does not improve one of those answers, it should not compete for top-level attention.

## 3. Organize Around Jobs, Not Routes

People think in terms like:

- review approvals
- investigate findings
- replenish stock
- connect data
- manage the team

They do not think in terms of disconnected route names.

## 4. One Primary Action Per Zone

Every section should have an obvious next move.

- one primary CTA
- supporting secondary actions
- tertiary utilities de-emphasized

Avoid toolbars where every button appears equally important.

## 5. Workflows Should Share One Grammar

Decisions, findings, alerts, approvals, and drafts should feel related.

Common patterns:

- severity
- scope
- evidence
- confidence
- recommended action
- measured outcome

## 6. Simplify Before Beautifying

Premium polish is not ornamental complexity.

Prefer:

- fewer surfaces
- clearer sectioning
- shorter labels
- stronger defaults
- deliberate whitespace

Reject:

- decorative data walls
- multiple competing hero moments
- vague AI cards without purpose

## 7. Role Scope Is A Product Feature

Role adaptation should feel intentional, not merely hidden.

- executives get synthesis
- regional managers get comparison and routing
- store managers get urgent operational tasks
- admins get governance and policy clarity
- developers get precision and contract confidence

## 8. Trust Must Be One Move Away

Security, audit, billing, tenant status, and trust posture should be easy to locate without searching.

Enterprise customers judge product maturity through these workflows as much as through analytics.

## 9. AI Must Behave Like A Reliable Colleague

Sage should:

- summarize clearly
- cite evidence
- recommend responsibly
- draft when allowed
- explain uncertainty

Sage should not:

- sound mystical
- hide scope boundaries
- imply unsupported autonomy
- overwhelm the user with verbosity

## 10. Dense Does Not Mean Cluttered

Enterprise pages may be information-rich, but hierarchy must stay obvious.

Use:

- grouped modules
- consistent table scaffolding
- sticky context where needed
- compact, readable copy

Avoid:

- mixed card families
- overuse of charts
- horizontally crowded forms

## 11. Design Every State

Production quality requires intentional treatment for:

- loading
- empty
- error
- degraded live connection
- no permission
- first-use / no data
- success / completed action

## 12. Accessibility Is Not A Cleanup Task

Accessibility must shape the first design, not the QA patch.

Required:

- keyboard-first operation
- focus visibility
- semantic landmarks
- readable contrast
- screen-reader-safe streaming and chat
- reduced motion support
- RTL-safe layout and copy rhythm

## 13. Put Personal Settings In The Utility Layer

Profile, preferences, language, and account actions belong in the utility/account area, not crowded into the main product map.

This keeps the primary navigation focused on business work.

## 14. Reserve Space For The Future Without Pretending It Exists

Future-ready design is good.
Future-fiction is not.

Use these labels consistently:

- `[Future Enhancement]`
- `[Requires New Backend Capability]`
- `[Reserved For Role Expansion]`
